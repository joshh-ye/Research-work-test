## Title

Leakage-aware evaluation of genomic foundation models for colorectal cancer risk-variant annotation and transcription-factor binding <!-- src: research_management/SCIENTIFIC_DECISIONS.md SD-020 (B title), SD-024 (Title = SD-020 B) -->

Note: this framing was chosen by the pre-registered rule (SD-024), not by the authors' preference.

## Abstract

**Background.** Fine-mapping of colorectal cancer (CRC) GWAS loci defines 5,741 credible causal variants (CCVs), mostly noncoding. Sequence-to-function models can score them, but benchmarks accounting for training-data exposure and independent functional data are lacking.
**Methods.** AlphaGenome, pretrained Borzoi, Enformer and Borzoi-TL (a frozen-trunk head on 183 CRC TF ChIP-seq tracks) were scored under one protocol frozen before any label was joined. The pre-registered primary test (P1) was within-locus stratified AUROC (not r, r^2 or R^2) for MPRA-active versus inactive variants (Law et al.; 130 positives, 2,518 negatives, 58 informative regions), with region-clustered bootstrap, permutation p, Holm over four models, and distance and annotation-count baselines. Track prediction was benchmarked on 359 test tiles against a ridge readout and a linear probe (TF-cluster bootstrap), with a training-exposure audit; a later program fine-tuned the Borzoi trunk (partial and low-rank) and evaluated six configurations once each under a committed test lock.
**Results.** P1 AUROC: Enformer 0.674 [0.607, 0.756], AlphaGenome 0.666 [0.590, 0.759], Borzoi-TL 0.661 [0.584, 0.768], pretrained Borzoi 0.640 [0.554, 0.744]; Holm p = 0.0004 for each (minimum attainable with 10,000 permutations). Distance to TSS gave 0.515 [0.445, 0.567] and the Chen annotation count 0.618 [0.556, 0.684]. All models exceeded distance; no model was shown to differ from another or to exceed the annotation-count baseline (all paired CIs include 0; absence of evidence, not equivalence). Direction concordance (P2) was below 0.5 but is uninterpretable: the MPRA sign convention is unresolved. For track prediction, a ridge readout and a linear probe exceeded Borzoi-TL on 152 non-identical tracks (TL minus ridge -0.0555 [-0.0756, -0.0428]; TL minus probe -0.0378 [-0.0525, -0.0289]; TF-cluster CIs). An eight-model trunk-fine-tuned ensemble with RC test-time averaging exceeded the E4 ridge (system versus system; test r 0.7578 versus 0.7406 on the 152 tracks; delta +0.0173, TF-cluster [0.0123, 0.0252], two-way tile-block x TF [0.0111, 0.0263]; 145/152 tracks improved). At least 31 of 183 test tracks are Borzoi/Enformer training experiments, and chr9/chr22 lie largely in their training folds.
**Conclusions.** Within loci, all four models rank MPRA-active variants above distance, not shown to differ from each other or from an annotation-count baseline; no tumour-specific signal was shown. The original frozen-trunk (head-only) transfer-learning recipe did not beat linear readouts; an ensemble of trunk-fine-tuned models with RC test-time averaging did, modestly, on test chromosomes that the pretrained trunks had partly seen. No causal or clinical claim is made.

## 1. Introduction

**1.1 CRC genetics.** [Colorectal cancer is heritable; GWAS have identified many risk loci (huyghe2019). Trans-ancestry fine-mapping of over 254,000 individuals defined 238 independent signals in 142 regions and 5,741 credible causal variants (chen2024); the CCV definition is variants whose conditional P is within two orders of magnitude of the signal's most significant variant (no posterior inclusion probability exists; `variant_provenance/PROVENANCE.md`). Most CCVs are noncoding. High-throughput MPRA has tested 8,880 candidate variants in colon cell lines (law2024).]
**1.2 Sequence-to-function models.** [Basenji (kelley2018), Enformer (avsec2021), Borzoi (linder2025) and AlphaGenome (avsec2026) predict chromatin, TF-binding and expression tracks from sequence and are used to score variants. Their training data (ENCODE, GEO, GTEx) overlap many public CRC assays; this makes test-set exposure a first-order concern for any downstream benchmark. Cross-individual variant-effect prediction is known to be limited (huang2023).]
**1.3 Transfer learning.** [Adapting a pretrained model to a disease-specific track panel by retraining a head has been shown for Enformer on breast and prostate cancer TF ChIP-seq (li2026) and parameter-efficient fine-tuning for Borzoi-family models has been described (yuan2026). Genomic augmentation is described in lee2023. Novelty of the method itself is limited; the contribution here is the leakage-aware evaluation, a supervised-baseline test of whether head-only (frozen-trunk) transfer learning exceeds a linear readout (it did not, for the one recipe tested), a locked test of whether a trunk-fine-tuned ensemble does (it did, modestly; Section 3.7.5), and the variant-annotation validation.]
**1.4 Aims.** [(i) Benchmark a frozen-trunk Borzoi head (Borzoi-TL) against pretrained Borzoi, Enformer and AlphaGenome and against supervised linear readouts (ridge on pretrained outputs; linear probe on the trunk embedding) on 183 CRC TF tracks with exposure-stratified inference. (ii) Score CRC CCVs with the same models under one frozen protocol and test, within locus, whether scores discriminate MPRA-active from inactive variants beyond trivial baselines. (iii) After the negative result of (i), test under a pre-committed lock whether fine-tuning the Borzoi trunk (partial or low-rank) improves on the pre-specified E4 ridge readout.]
Note for authors: the Introduction sentences above are outlines. Only the citations in `references.tsv` may be used; no other reference has been verified.

### 2.3 Tiles and splits
The genome is cut into non-overlapping 524,288 bp windows from position 0 (`borzoi_code/genome_tiler.py`). Training: all other assembled chromosomes (4,659 tiles); validation chr1 + chr8 (750 tiles); test chr9 + chr22 (359 tiles) <!-- exposure_audit/EXPOSURE_AUDIT.md §4 (tile counts reconstructed from hg38.ml.fa.fai; 359 matches the known test count) -->. Targets are the 32 bp-binned signal over the central 131,072 bp (4,096 bins); inputs are one-hot 524,288 bp <!-- borzoi_code/dataset.py:10 -->. Evaluation on a shared 128 bp grid (4-bin means) is used for cross-model comparison; native-grid (32 bp) numbers are reported separately and never mixed with shared-grid numbers.
**These chromosomes are not held out from the pretrained backbones.** Test chr9/chr22 tiles: 43.6% of bp lie in Borzoi training folds (30.4% in the Borzoi test fold) and 73.0% in Enformer training folds; validation tiles chr1/chr8: 65.7% (Borzoi) and 89.7% (Enformer) in training folds <!-- exposure_audit/EXPOSURE_AUDIT.md §4 table; Enformer fold file is the third-party Genentech reproduction, Basenji2 first-party bed 0.726 per RV-004 -->. The chr9/chr22 test set is also development-exposed (results were viewed during development; SD-009).

### 2.4 Borzoi-TL architecture and training (as actually run)
Four pretrained Borzoi replicates (`johahi/borzoi-replicate-0..3`, HF port via `borzoi-pytorch`) are frozen; the 1,920-channel trunk embedding that feeds `human_head` is captured with a forward pre-hook, averaged across the four replicates, centre-cropped to 4,096 bins, and passed to the only trainable module: `Linear(1920,1024) -> GELU -> Dropout(0.2) -> Linear(1024,183) -> Softplus` (2,154,679 parameters) <!-- borzoi_code/model.py; PHASE1_RECOVERY_REPORT.md §4 -->.
Training (Slurm 12212537, `main.py --epochs 8 --ckpt-dir ./borzoi_ckpt_full --output-dir ./results_full`, `training_run.slurm`): Adam on head parameters, lr 1e-4, weight decay 1e-4, batch size 1, Poisson negative log-likelihood (`log_input=False`), `ReduceLROnPlateau` (factor 0.5, patience 1), early-stopping patience 3, augmentation on training tiles only: random shift up to +/-128 bp snapped to whole 32 bp bins (+/-4 bins) and reverse-complement with p = 0.5 <!-- main.py:62-85, 265-282; borzoi_code/dataset.py:27-53 -->. **Not used:** inverse-signal per-track loss weighting (`--balance-tracks` was not passed), and no low-variance down-weighting (`low_variance_flag` is False for all 183 tracks) <!-- research_management/EXPERIMENT_REGISTRY.csv EXP-001; PHASE1_RECOVERY_REPORT.md §9 -->. The checkpoint used is epoch 5 of 8 (validation NLL 0.18523; epochs 6-8 did not improve) <!-- borzoi_ckpt_full/ (epoch 5); results_full/history.json -->. **The headline checkpoint is a single run; no seed was set; single train/val/test split.** Seed and chromosome-fold robustness were assessed afterwards with heads retrained on a slim cache whose recipe differs from the headline checkpoint (Section 2.5b). The choice of trunk embedding as the feature tap was not ablated; the same embedding is used by the linear probe (Section 2.5a). The Poisson-NLL MLP head, batch size 1, lr 1e-4, 8-epoch recipe is the only head-only (frozen-trunk) transfer-learning recipe evaluated on chr9/chr22; within the E4 phase no alternative loss, optimiser, batch size or head architecture was evaluated there (SD-021) <!-- src: research_management/SCIENTIFIC_DECISIONS.md SD-020, SD-021 -->. Trunk fine-tuning was evaluated later, in a separate program with its own test lock (Section 2.8c).

### 2.5a Supervised linear baselines (E4; EXP-013; rule pre-specified in SD-008, committed before results)

- **Linear probe (exposure-symmetric comparison).** Same ridge on the frozen 1,920-channel trunk embedding that the Borzoi-TL head consumes (`probe:trunk1920`), so it sees the same input as TL and no pretrained output vocabulary. <!-- src: results_phase2/e4_ridge/SUMMARY.md; research_management/REVIEW_FINDINGS.md RV-006 (probe to be foregrounded) -->

Selected lambda 1e-4 for ridge[pre_raw:union1888] and 0.01 for the probe, neither at a grid edge. <!-- src: results_phase2/e4_sensitivity/SUMMARY.md part B (code read of 03_ridge.py / ridgelib.py); results_phase2/e4_ridge/SUMMARY.md lambda table -->

- **Exposure.** The ridge features (1,888 channels) include Borzoi training tracks for >=31 of the 183 targets, so ridge and TL are stratified by Borzoi exposure class; the probe is the exposure-symmetric comparator. <!-- src: results_phase2/e4_ridge/SUMMARY.md limitations -->

### 2.5b Seed and chromosome-fold robustness (E5; EXP-015) and held-out-vocabulary transfer (E8; EXP-016)
- **Cache recipe (SD-018, storage-forced deviation).** The slim trunk cache keeps forward trunk embeddings, targets and pretrained Borzoi outputs for 1,888 channels; reverse-complement embeddings were not kept. Heads retrained for E5 and E8 therefore use the "cache recipe": identical hyperparameters to the headline run except (i) no reverse-complement augmentation, (ii) shift augmentation emulated on the cached embeddings, (iii) fp16 cached features. The original checkpoint remains the headline TL model; it was re-evaluated from the fp16 cache as a gate and reproduces the published statistics. <!-- src: research_management/SCIENTIFIC_DECISIONS.md SD-018; results_phase2/e5_seeds/SUMMARY.md; results_phase2/e4_ridge/SUMMARY.md (gate rows) -->
- **Seeds.** Five heads with explicit seeds 1-5 on the original split. Seeds vary head initialisation, dropout and data order/augmentation only; the frozen backbone and its exposure to chr9/chr22 are unchanged. <!-- src: results_phase2/e5_seeds/SUMMARY.md -->
- **Chromosome folds.** Folds F2 (test chr6, chr21), F3 (chr11, chr19, chr20) and F4 (chr7, chr18), each with its own validation chromosomes used only for early stopping, lambda and pretrained-track selection. Fold CV tests head robustness only; it does not remove backbone sequence exposure (SD-009 clarification). All nine fold x seed heads are complete (F2, F3, F4 x seeds 1-3; cache recipe). No linear-probe statistics exist for F2-F4. <!-- src: results_phase2/e5_folds/e5_fold_vs_ridge.csv (9 heads per track set) -->
- **E8 design (validation-selected same-TF transfer; not zero-shot).** One head (seed 1, cache recipe) is trained on 166 tracks, excluding the 17 tracks of GP5d, LS174T, LS180 and DLD-1. For each held-out track, a trained same-TF track of another cell line is selected using the held-out track's own validation-chromosome (chr1, chr8) labels (fallback rule: any trained track, 4 tracks), then linearly calibrated on validation; this is compared with the pretrained Borzoi validation-selected mapping (1,882 any-TF ChIP channels). Because validation labels of the held-out tracks are used, this is not zero-shot, and the selection freedom of the two arms differs. <!-- src: results_phase2/e8_generalisation/SUMMARY.md -->

### 2.7 Metrics
Three quantities are distinct and always labelled:
- **Pearson r** (primary): per-track correlation over all test bins (pooled over tiles), averaged over tracks.
- **Squared Pearson r (r^2)**: the square of that correlation (per track, then averaged).
- **Prediction R^2 = 1 - SS_res/SS_tot** on the raw scale. For pretrained models this is **validation-calibrated R^2** (a linear map fitted on validation chromosomes, then applied to test); uncalibrated raw-scale R^2 of pretrained tracks is meaningless (previously observed -193) and is not reported. TL R^2 is uncalibrated on test, so TL-minus-baseline R^2 differences compare unlike objects and are secondary.
MPRA validation uses a different quantity, the stratified within-region AUROC, which is not r, r^2 or R^2. 51 of 359 test tiles have zero signal and inflate pooled correlations; a sensitivity analysis excludes them.

### 2.8 Bootstrap designs for model comparison
Inference units (SD-006): the **primary** inference unit is the **TF cluster**, on the primary analysis set of 152 non-identical-experiment tracks (`not_identical152`; all 183 secondary); the cell-line-cluster and tile-block CIs are reported alongside <!-- src: research_management/SCIENTIFIC_DECISIONS.md SD-006, SD-019, SD-023; RV-002 modification -->. Delta r = model A minus model B (tracks matched), 10,000 resamples, seed 20261020, percentile 95% CI <!-- results_phase2/exposure_stratified/SUMMARY.md; results_phase2/e4_sensitivity/SUMMARY.md part A -->: (a) track resampling (descriptive only; tracks are not independent); (b) **TF-cluster** (77 clusters; 62 for the 152-track set); (c) **cell-line-cluster** (12 labels incl. two tissue labels; 11 for the 152-track set); (d) **tile-block** (37 blocks of 10 contiguous tiles, within chromosome; only two chromosomes, so chromosome-level resampling is impossible); (e) **two-way** tile-block x cluster, with both models recomputed per resample. Cluster-level sign-flip tests on cluster-mean deltas (exact for <=16 clusters). Subsets with <6 clusters are descriptive only. Cell-line-cluster CIs rest on only 12 (11) clusters and are wide. For E4/E5, paired TL-minus-ridge and TL-minus-probe CIs use the same resampling indices and gate against the primary E4 tile-block CIs to 5e-5 <!-- src: results_phase2/e4_sensitivity/SUMMARY.md part A gate -->. The pretrained R^2 calibration is fixed and not resampled. The sign-flip test (unweighted mean of cluster means) and the CIs (track-weighted mean) estimate different quantities.

### 2.8b Post-primary-result sensitivity analyses (E4; EXP-014)
Two analyses were specified (SD-023) after the primary E4 result was known and are therefore labelled post-primary-result sensitivity analyses; they cannot overturn the pre-registered outcome (SD-019). <!-- src: research_management/SCIENTIFIC_DECISIONS.md SD-023; results_phase2/e4_sensitivity/SUMMARY.md; RV-006 #4 -->
- **Exposure-controlled ridge.** For each target, remove from the 1,888 feature channels every Borzoi track that is the identical experiment (same GSM or ENCSR) or a ChIP track of the same TF in the same cell line; targets with no such channel keep all channels; one lambda for all tracks chosen on validation. This is partial exposure control only: channels of other TFs and assays in the same cell line remain.
- **Fold stratification of test tiles.** Test tiles are split by overlap with Borzoi validation+test windows (`sequences_human.bed.gz`; held-out tile = >=50% of the evaluated central 131,072 bp in those windows; classifying by the full 524,288 bp input window gives identical membership, 0 tiles differ <!-- src: analysis/priorityD/07_e4_sensitivity.py part D (primary = centre131072; full524288 computed as alternative); results_phase2/e4_sensitivity SUMMARY -->) and per-track r is recomputed per group. Only Borzoi folds are used; Enformer fold exposure is not assessed here.

### 2.8c Trunk fine-tuning program (EXP-019; after the E4 result; own pre-specified test lock)
This program was specified after the pre-registered E4 outcome (SD-019) was known, in `research_management/finetuning/FT_PLAN.md` (written 2026-10-23 before any fine-tuning experiment), and asks a different question from Section 2.4: whether updating Borzoi trunk weights improves on the pre-specified E4 linear readout, ridge[pre_raw:union1888]. It is not part of the E2 pre-registration. SD-021 restricted new head architectures on chr9/chr22 during the E4 phase because the test set is development-exposed; running a separately locked program afterwards is recorded in SD-029 and `FT_PLAN.md`, with the committed lock as the mitigation for forking paths; it evaluated six configurations on the same development-exposed chr9/chr22 test set (SD-009) under the lock described below. <!-- src: research_management/finetuning/FT_PLAN.md; LOCK_PROPOSAL.md; research_management/EXPERIMENT_REGISTRY.csv EXP-019 -->
- **Terminology (used consistently in this manuscript).** The regimes below are distinguished by which weights are trained.
  - *Frozen-trunk transfer learning* (Borzoi-TL, Section 2.4; FT-S01/FT-S07 controls; cache MLP heads FT-C83/C84): all Borzoi trunk weights are fixed and only a new nonlinear head is trained. It is also called "head-only".
  - *Linear readouts*: ridge regression on frozen representations. The linear probe (`probe:trunk1920`) reads the trunk embedding. The E4 ridge and FT-C61 read the pretrained output tracks, FT-C61 also using the trunk centre bin.
  - *Partial fine-tuning* (partial-4): a subset of the original trunk weights is updated together with a new head.
  - *Parameter-efficient fine-tuning* (LoRA r16): the original trunk weights stay frozen and low-rank adapter weights inserted into the trunk are trained with a new head. This changes the trunk's function but not its stored pretrained weights.
  - *Full fine-tuning*: every trunk weight is updated. It was run only as a validation-only development arm (FT-S05). It is not a member of any test-evaluated configuration.
  - *Ensemble*: an unweighted mean of separately trained models' predictions, unless a weight is stated.
  
  The locked primary is an ensemble of four partial-fine-tuned and four LoRA models. It contains no full fine-tuning and no frozen-trunk or linear component. <!-- src: results_ft/runs/<run>/run_cmd.json; research_management/finetuning/weight_change_check.json (all 8 members: partial-4 all 82 trunk tensors differ from pretrained, median relative change 0.017-0.020; LoRA 53/53 non-zero lora_B); reports/FINETUNING_VALIDATION_AUDIT.md -->
- **Fixed evaluation contract.** Splits, tiles, the 183 targets, the 896 x 128 bp shared grid and the per-track pooled-correlation code path are those of E4 (Sections 2.3, 2.5a); input context stayed 524,288 bp. The E4 ridge was re-computed through the same pipeline as a gate and reproduces its published test statistics (mean r difference < 1e-10). <!-- src: reports/FINETUNING_FINAL_REPORT.md §2; results_ft/final/test_summary.csv (E4_ridge_repro_pooled128 rows) -->
- **Model.** Each fine-tuned model uses one pretrained Borzoi replicate trunk (`johahi/borzoi-replicate-N`, N = 0-3; `borzoi-pytorch` 0.4.4; HF revisions in `research_management/CHECKPOINT_INVENTORY.md`), not the four-replicate averaged embedding of Borzoi-TL. ## 4. Discussion
1. **Principal finding (variant annotation; Framing B, pre-registered).** Within Chen fine-mapping regions, all four models rank MPRA-active CCVs above MPRA-inactive CCVs better than chance (stratified AUROC 0.640-0.674, Holm p 0.0004 for each) and better than distance to the nearest TSS (0.5153, at chance) <!-- src: results_phase2/e2_validation/final/tables/p1.tsv, baselines.tsv -->. This is consistent with the models capturing sequence features of regulatory activity that prioritise variants within a locus beyond proximity to a gene, in a design (within-region, region-clustered) that guards against the trivial between-locus confound. The effect is modest, the CIs are wide (lower bounds 0.554-0.607), and the check is against an episomal assay in three colon lines.
2. **What the models do not show.** (a) *No model differences:* the six headline pairwise CIs all include 0, and the study can resolve only differences of a few hundredths (minimum detectable AUROC 0.5834 pre-unblinding, 0.5847 recomputed in the final run, against 0.5); the ordering Enformer, AlphaGenome, Borzoi-TL, pretrained Borzoi is not evidence that any model is better. A second reason, besides power, is that the primary scores are maxima over different numbers of tracks (152 Borzoi-TL, 132 pretrained Borzoi, 33 AlphaGenome, 132 Enformer; Section 2.10), so model comparisons confound model quality with track-set size and composition (the mean-|score| sensitivity partly addresses this). (b) *Comparable to existing annotation:* the Chen annotation count alone gives 0.6182 [0.5560, 0.6845], and no model is shown to exceed it (all four paired CIs include 0), so added value over existing epigenomic annotation is not demonstrated; the models may nonetheless be recapitulating the same regulatory information. The pre-registered paired difference in AUROC versus the annotation count and the post hoc conditional-logit coefficient of the score with annotation count as a covariate (3.5.3) answer different questions on the same data; the latter is suggestive only and was not designed or powered to establish added value. (c) *No tumour specificity:* HCEC-1CT (normal colon) labels give AUROCs similar to the tumour lines HT29 and SW403, so no tumour or "CRC-specific" signal is shown and the models are not claimed to annotate CRC specifically. (d) *Direction unresolved:* P2 concordance is below 0.5 for all four models, but with the ST4 sign convention unresolved this is uninterpretable and is not used to infer the REF/ALT convention; AlphaGenome colon RNA-seq direction against GTEx eQTL slopes is at chance overall (0.5008), without a join error, and tail-subset increases are post hoc and not a finding. (e) *Weight concentration:* 45.0% of the P1 weight sits in three regions; equal-weight and leave-region-out analyses (post hoc exploratory) give similar or higher AUROC, so the result is not driven by those regions, but about 12 effective regions support the estimate.
3. **Track benchmark as a cautionary, leakage-aware result.** For the original head-only (frozen-trunk) transfer-learning recipe (Poisson-NLL MLP head on the frozen Borzoi trunk, batch size 1, lr 1e-4, 8 epochs), a ridge readout of pretrained Borzoi outputs and a linear probe on the same trunk embedding both exceed Borzoi-TL on held-out chromosomes (primary inference TF-cluster, 152 non-identical tracks; all CIs exclude 0 across tile-block, TF-cluster and cell-line-cluster units), robustly over five head seeds and folds F2-F4, within every Borzoi exposure class, and after partial exposure control of the ridge (Supplementary Results SR4). <!-- src: results_phase2/e4_sensitivity/A_cluster_cis.csv; e5_seeds; e5_folds; SD-019 --> This was the pre-registered SD-008 outcome and withdraws any claim of Borzoi-TL superiority or CRC-specific adaptation (SD-019). TL minus pretrained Borzoi is small and not robust to cell-line clustering, and held-out-vocabulary transfer shows no advantage (Supplementary Results SR3). The result is scoped to the single recipe tested: the probe-versus-TL gap could arise from loss, optimisation, batch size or head nonlinearity, none of which was tested on chr9/chr22, and no statement is made about transfer learning in general (trunk fine-tuning, tested later, is item 3a). Its broader lesson is methodological: at least 31 of 183 test tracks are Borzoi/Enformer training experiments and chr9/chr22 lie largely inside their training folds, so evaluations on public CRC panels must report exposure explicitly.
3a. **A trunk-fine-tuned ensemble exceeds the E4 ridge, modestly (EXP-019; Section 3.7.5).** In a later program with its own committed test lock, an ensemble of eight fine-tuned Borzoi models (four partial-trunk models, each updating the last four transformer blocks and the U-Net decoder, and four LoRA models, each adding rank-16 adapters to a frozen trunk; one of each per pretrained replicate; new MLP head; RC test-time averaging) reached test r 0.7578 versus 0.7406 for the E4 ridge on the 152 non-identical tracks (delta r +0.0173; TF-cluster [0.0123, 0.0252], two-way tile-block x TF [0.0111, 0.0263]), meeting the pre-specified rule; 145 of 152 tracks improved. <!-- src: research_management/finetuning/final/test_ci_table.csv; test_summary.csv --> Together with the E4 result this narrows the earlier conclusion: the original head-only recipe did not beat a linear readout, but the locked fine-tuned system does. What produces the gain is not established. On validation, unfreezing added about +0.009 over a matched frozen head on the same replicate-0 trunk, but a frozen-feature Poisson MLP on the cached trunk embedding (FT-C83, cache recipe, validation early stopping) reached 0.7378, comparable to a single partial-4 model (0.7373) and above the E4 ridge (0.7337). The evidence therefore does not establish that trunk adaptation is necessary to exceed a linear readout; the test gain belongs to the locked system as a whole (trunk updating, replicate ensembling and RC averaging; Section 3.7.5). The gain is a ChIP-track result only, and the SD-019 withdrawal of any "CRC-specific adaptation" claim stands. The size of the gain matters for interpretation. +0.017 in mean Pearson r, about +0.024 in r^2 and in prediction R^2, is small in absolute terms, and the median per-track gain (+0.011) is smaller than the mean because the gains concentrate in tracks the ridge predicts poorly (for example GATA6 in Caco-2). The improvement is consistent across cell lines and almost all TFs, but it is a quantitative improvement in ChIP-track prediction, not evidence of a qualitatively new capability. It has not been shown to improve variant scoring; no fine-tuned model was used in the MPRA analysis. Remaining uncertainty: (i) about 44% of test base pairs are in Borzoi training folds, and both systems' trunks were pretrained on them and on the 31 identical experiments, so the gain is shown on chr9/chr22 as they are, not on sequence unseen by pretrained Borzoi; the bias from pretrained-head exposure plausibly favours the ridge, which is why the 152-track set is primary, and the post hoc smaller gain on the 31 identical tracks (+0.0094) is consistent with that but not a test of it; (ii) only two test chromosomes, so the absolute r carries a tile-block CI of about +/-0.02 and the TF-cluster CI is conditional on these regions; (iii) the chr9/chr22 test set was already development-exposed (SD-009) and the E4 numbers were public before the lock, although every fine-tuning selection used validation chromosomes only; (iv) how much of the gain is trunk updating, ensembling or RC averaging was not separated on test. (v) Robustness. A post hoc ablation, selected on validation, showed that a two-member ensemble matched the primary's gain at a quarter of the inference cost (Section 3.7.6). However, this held only because the primary contains a failed member: the pair was about 0.002 below the four- to six-member ensembles without it. One member (FT-L2) failed on a contiguous 12 Mb test region that validation did not reveal. The equal-weight ensemble absorbed this failure, and excluding that post hoc region changed the primary's gain only slightly (+0.0178 versus +0.0173). Even so, a member can behave normally on validation chromosomes yet fail on others. Ensembling is a safeguard here, and region-level quality control of members should precede any use of single fine-tuned models.
4. **Training exposure as a design issue.** Public CRC ChIP-seq (especially LoVo GSE51142 and ENCODE colon/HCT-116) is in the pretraining vocabularies; fold-level overlap of any "held-out" chromosome with backbone training must be reported. For the variant analysis, pretrained models saw the REF sequence of all loci and ENCODE/GTEx data; the MPRA labels are independent measurements, but the Law tiers used ATAC/ChIP annotations, GTEx RNA-seq is in model training, and AlphaGenome training exposure could not be verified. The chr1/8/9/22 subset is Borzoi-TL validation/test chromosomes only and is not a leakage test.
5. **Relation to prior work.** The closest work is li2026 (Enformer retrained on breast and prostate TF ChIP-seq, with variant and TWAS analyses); method novelty here is limited, and the added value is exposure-stratified inference, a four-model comparison under one frozen protocol, and region-clustered MPRA validation. law2024 did not report Enformer-, Borzoi- or AlphaGenome-type variant scoring of their MPRA (their only deep-learning model is Akita, for 3D contact prediction) <!-- src: Europe PMC fullTextXML PMC11525171 grep, 2026-10-20 -->; hu2026 applied AlphaGenome to four CRC lead variants without a multi-model comparison or MPRA benchmark. To our knowledge (web search, 2026-10-20; not exhaustive) a CRC-specific multi-model MPRA benchmark has not been reported; general MPRA/caQTL benchmarks of these models exist (avsec2026). Cross-individual variant-effect prediction is known to be limited (huang2023); the present within-locus, between-variant design is a different question.
6. **Interpretation.** Associations with MPRA activity at fine-mapped loci do not establish causal variants or mechanisms; larger predicted effects do not imply greater accuracy; Borzoi scores are on the scaled output scale, so magnitudes are not comparable across models.
7. **Proposed future work, ranked by what each would decide (not done; proposals only).** (i) *Resolve the ST4 logFC sign convention with the MPRA authors (BL-007; Law/Houlston), highest priority because it is cheap and decides whether P2 is interpretable at all.* P2 was specified assuming the ALT/REF direction; if the ST4 convention were REF/ALT, the pre-registered P2 fractions would correspond to 1 minus the reported values, that is, hypothetically AlphaGenome 0.6894 (91/132), pretrained Borzoi 0.7273 (96/132), Borzoi-TL 0.6742 (89/132) and Enformer 0.6364 (84/132). These are arithmetic complements (1 - frac from `p2.tsv`), a hypothetical, not a result: no CI, p value or claim is derived from them, and P2 would be re-reported as a new, clearly labelled analysis only after the convention is confirmed. <!-- src: results_phase2/e2_validation/final/tables/p2.tsv (frac 0.3106, 0.2727, 0.3258, 0.3636; complements computed here as 1 - frac with n = 132) --> (ii) *Validate in an independent MPRA, STARR or caQTL dataset with a pre-specified nested test (model plus annotation versus annotation alone) and an adequately powered tumour-versus-normal design.* This would decide whether the models add information beyond annotation counts, which neither the pre-registered paired difference nor the post hoc conditional logit can settle; candidates are listed in `VALIDATION_AVAILABILITY.md` but were not retrieved or verified here and are not cited. No further confirmatory analyses on the Law MPRA are planned. (iii) *Obtain the AlphaGenome training manifest*, which would decide whether AlphaGenome results are exposure-contaminated (currently unverifiable). (iv) *Open questions after the trunk fine-tuning program (the program itself is reported in item 3a and Section 3.7.5):* (a) a Borzoi-fold-stratified analysis of the fine-tuning gain, which would show whether it differs between test tiles inside and outside Borzoi training folds; it is computable from the existing per-tile statistics with the E4 part-D procedure (SD-023) and was not done, and such tiles are still not held out from Enformer; a test on sequence unseen by any pretrained trunk would need a newly pretrained trunk; (b) a factorial test of trunk updating, frozen-feature heads, replicate ensembling and RC averaging on independent regions, which would decide what produces the gain; (c) variant scoring with the fine-tuned ensemble under a new pre-specified amendment, which would decide whether better track prediction carries over to MPRA discrimination (not run; any such analysis would be outside the frozen E2 plan).

## 5. Limitations (merged list; RV-007 items and earlier items deduplicated)
Test/data (track benchmark)
1. Single split for the headline analysis; the headline checkpoint is one unseeded run. Seeds 1-5 and folds F2-F4 use heads retrained on a slim cache (no RC, shift-emulated, fp16; SD-018), so they test a slightly different recipe; all nine fold x seed heads (F2/F3/F4 x seeds 1-3) are complete. No linear-probe statistics exist for F2-F4. No ablation of any recipe component and no alternative head architecture was evaluated in the E4 phase (SD-021; trunk fine-tuning was evaluated later, Limitations 23-27). The negative result is scoped to the original head-only (frozen-trunk) transfer-learning recipe, and loss/optimisation as its explanation is untested. <!-- src: SD-018, SD-020, SD-021; results_phase2/e5_folds/SUMMARY.md -->
2. chr9/chr22 are development-exposed (SD-009) and about 44% (Borzoi) / 73% (Enformer) of test bp lie in pretrained training folds; chr1/chr8 validation also overlaps (66% / 90%). Chromosome-fold CV tests head robustness only, not backbone exposure. The Enformer fold file is a third-party reproduction; Borzoi HF replicate weights are assumed to share the README folds (unverified).
3. Region-level uncertainty in the track benchmark spans two test chromosomes; chromosome-level resampling is impossible.
4. The 31 identical-experiment tracks are a lower bound (GSM/ENCSR identity only; ENCODE cross-references searched for HCT116/Caco-2 only; reprocessed duplicates missed). AlphaGenome training exposure is undetermined (manifest not obtained); the AlphaGenome exclusion is a proxy.
5. Oracle mapping selects on test (upper bound); validation-selected mapping is deployable. The earlier 1,882-track "filtered Borzoi" comparison used test-selected tracks.
6. Non-independence: 183 tracks, 77 TFs, 12 cell-line/tissue labels (11 for the 152-track set); cell-line-cluster CIs rest on few clusters and are wide; identical-experiment, same-cell-line-same-target and unexposed subsets have <6 clusters and are descriptive. The sign-flip test and the CIs estimate different quantities. The 17 "unexposed" cell-line tracks test pretrained exposure, not TL generalisation. E8 rests on 4 cell lines (about 3 donor clusters: LS174T is recorded as a derivative of LS180, cellosaurus), with 94% of the gap in four LS180 tracks, a non-selection-matched comparison and the held-out tracks' validation labels used (not zero-shot).
7. Exposure control is partial: ridge features include Borzoi outputs for training tracks of at least 31 targets; the exposure-controlled ridge removes only identical-experiment and same-cell-line same-TF channels (53 tracks affected); the linear probe is the exposure-symmetric comparison; Enformer fold exposure was not assessed in the E4 sensitivity analysis. The exposure-controlled ridge and the Borzoi fold stratification were specified after the primary E4 result; fold-stratified groups are confounded with chromosome (held-out tiles 96% chr9) and signal level.
8. 51 zero-signal tiles inflate pooled Pearson r; r, r^2 and R^2 are not interchangeable; TL R^2 (uncalibrated) versus baseline validation-calibrated R^2 compare unlike objects.
9. Cell-type, resolution and context mismatch between models (524 kb/32 bp, 196 kb/128 bp, 1 Mb); the pretrained 132-track set is LoVo-heavy (118 LoVo) whereas the Borzoi-TL and AlphaGenome sets are HCT-116-heavy, and max-over-tracks favours models with more tracks (mean-|score| sensitivity pre-specified).
10. Enformer snapshot revision used in the August oracle run is unknown; the AlphaGenome hosted API varies slightly between calls (max 2.1e-4 in per-target r).
Variants and MPRA validation
11. S6 contains no alleles; alleles come from marginal trans-ancestry GWAS summary statistics (GCST90129505, harmonised with Ensembl 95/dbSNP 151), not Chen's conditional analyses. "Matched" certifies the tested allele pair only; the risk allele is by beta sign; 544 palindromic variants outside S15 rely on the harmonisation strand; 10 CCVs (23 rows) without allele data are excluded, not guessed; two rsID-drift CCVs are lost.
12. The upstream hg19-to-hg38 conversion that built the scoring input is undocumented (verified post hoc by liftover and Ensembl). The 4,396 extra ALTs at matched positions are exploratory.
13. **The MPRA is episomal** and measures activity in three colon lines (HT29, SW403, HCEC-1CT), not chromatin context. **A non-hit is not a null**: negatives are "tested, ST2 flag 0", per-variant effect sizes of non-hits are not public, and low activity or low power cannot be separated from no effect. Flag-1 variants are ambiguous and excluded from the primary.
14. **Few effective regions.** P1 uses 58 informative regions with concentrated weight (top 3 regions 45.0% of weight; effective number of regions 11.9; post hoc exploratory); LD within loci and about 143 independent regions overall; minimum detectable AUROC 0.5834 pre-unblinding (0.5847 recomputed in the final run). Differences between models of a few hundredths cannot be resolved ("no model differs" refers to the six headline pairs and is not equivalence).
15. **The annotation-count baseline matches the models** (0.6182 [0.5560, 0.6845]); no model is shown to exceed it, so added value over existing annotation is not shown.
16. **No tumour-versus-normal specificity**: HCEC-1CT (normal) labels give AUROCs similar to the tumour lines; no "CRC-specific" annotation claim is made.
17. **P2 is unresolved**: the Law ST4 logFC sign convention is unknown (SD-017, BL-007); the below-0.5 concordance is uninterpretable, is not used to infer the REF/ALT convention, and is excluded from the framing rule. The only literature check available (one variant, rs6983267, ST4 REF G / ALT T) is weak: the risk allele G binds TCF4 more strongly (tuupanen2009) and enhances linked c-MYC expression (wright2010), which would suggest a positive ST4 logFC means REF higher, but this rests on a single variant and an episomal assay, was read from abstracts only, and was not used to re-sign P2 <!-- src: external_data/validation/MPRA_SIGN_CONVENTION.md lines 15-20; manuscript/references.tsv -->.
18. **Pretrained models saw all loci**: the REF sequence of every locus and ENCODE/GTEx data were in the training data of the pretrained models (Borzoi and Enformer; AlphaGenome unverified); the chr1/8/9/22 subset is not held out and is underpowered (19 positives, 10 regions), so it neither supports nor refutes leakage. eQTL checks are only partly independent (GTEx RNA-seq is in training; GTEx v10 sign concordance with ALT/REF 0.57, p 0.17, gives no sign information <!-- src: external_data/validation/MPRA_SIGN_CONVENTION.md (67/118 = 0.568, p 0.17) -->).
19. **Score conventions**: AlphaGenome quantile scores are provider-defined and tie-heavy (97.5% ties; 254 distinct values over all rows; midranks; raw log2 is the tie-free sensitivity); AlphaGenome RC averaging is server-side. Borzoi scores are summed in the scaled/soft-clipped output scale, and pseudocount conventions (pc = 1 on aggregated sums; server-side epsilon for AlphaGenome) differ between models. 512 bp versus 501 bp windows and bin granularity (Enformer 128 bp bins blur the variant position) differ across models. Chromosome-end windows: 149 (Borzoi) / 30 (Enformer) rows N-padded, 242 AlphaGenome rows shifted (S4 excludes them).
20. **Post hoc analyses** (EXP-018) were defined after the final unblinding, use the same data, and have no multiplicity control; the clogit likelihood-ratio p values assume independent variants and are not quoted as evidence strength; eQTL tail-subset concordances are not findings.
